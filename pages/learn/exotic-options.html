1. Asian Options (Average Price) Payoff depends on the arithmetic or geometric average price (Sˉ\bar{S}Sˉ) over the contract period: max⁡(Sˉ−K,0)\max(\bar{S} - K, 0)max(Sˉ−K,0). Ideal for hedging continuous monthly FX revenues or commodity purchases across a fiscal year. Cheaper than vanilla because averaging dampens volatility (σavg≈σ/3\sigma_{\text{avg}} \approx \sigma / \sqrt{3}σavg​≈σ/3​). Prevents manipulation on expiration day.
2. Barrier Options (Knock-In / Knock-Out) Option either ceases to exist (Knock-Out) or springs into existence (Knock-In) if spot StS_tSt​ touches a barrier level HHH. Cuts hedging premium by 40%+40\%+40%+ if the hedger has a firm view that prices won't breach HHH. Discontinuous Greeks: Gamma and Vega explode to infinity near the barrier HHH, creating extreme dealer hedging risk.
3. Binary / Digital Options Pays a fixed lump sum $Q\$Q$Q if ST>KS_T > KST​>K (Cash-or-Nothing), or $0\$0$0 otherwise. Used in structured retail notes, event prediction markets, and executive bonus triggers. Delta and Gamma invert near expiry; replicates an infinitely tight vertical call spread.
4. Lookback Options Holder buys at the lowest price (Smin⁡S_{\min}Smin​) or sells at the highest price (Smax⁡S_{\max}Smax​) achieved during the option's life. Eliminates market-timing regret ("perfect hindsight"). Extremely expensive upfront premium; rarely cost-effective for corporate hedging.
5. Compound & Rainbow Options Compound: An option on an option (e.g., Call on a Call).
Rainbow: Payoff depends on 2+ correlated assets.		 Used for phased R&D projects, M&A bidding contingencies, and dual-commodity power generation spreads. Requires multi-dimensional Monte Carlo or bivariate normal copulas; highly sensitive to correlation (ρ\rhoρ).

⚠️ Common Misconceptions
  • Misconception 1: "A Knock-Out Option is a safe, cheap substitute for a standard protective put."
    Reality: Consider a Down-and-Out Put with strike K=$100K = \$100K=$100 and knock-out barrier H=$80H = \$80H=$80. It protects you against a gentle decline from $100\$100$100 to $81\$81$81, but the exact moment a market crash hits $80\$80$80, your insurance policy vanishes into thin air right when you need it most! Never use knock-out barriers in the direction of your catastrophic tail risk.
  • Misconception 2: "Binary (Digital) options are easy to delta-hedge near expiration."
    Reality: One hour before expiration, if the stock is at $99.99\$99.99$99.99 on a $100.00\$100.00$100.00 binary option paying $1,000,000\$1,000,000$1,000,000, a $0.02\$0.02$0.02 tick changes the payout from $0\$0$0 to $1,000,000\$1,000,000$1,000,000. The theoretical Delta exceeds 50,000,00050,000,00050,000,000, making continuous delta-hedging impossible ("pin risk on steroids"). Dealers hedge binaries by over-hedging with tight vanilla vertical spreads.
📝 Notes for Future-Me

For corporate currency and commodity hedging, Asian Options are the gold standard; Barrier Options are usually a trap:

  • Always favor Asian Options: A multinational corporation does not earn 100% of its European revenue on December 31 at 4:00 PM—it earns revenue smoothly every week of the year. Hedging annual foreign cash flows with a single Asian Average-Rate Option is 35%+35\%+35%+ cheaper than buying a vanilla option and matches your actual cash flow timing.
  • Avoid Complex Structured Notes: When an investment bank pitches a "Zero-Cost Structured FX Accumulator" or "Target Redemption Forward (TARF)" loaded with knock-out barriers, walk away. Those structures give you small gains in calm markets and saddle you with 2×2\times2× leveraged losses when currencies break out.
