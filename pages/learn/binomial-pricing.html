Binomial Option Pricing Model

Primary: John C. Hull Ch. 13 · also: Sheldon Natenberg Ch. 15
Intuition

The Cox-Ross-Rubinstein (CRR, 1979) Binomial Option Pricing Model is a discrete-time lattice framework that models the price evolution of an underlying asset over a discrete number of steps until option expiration.

At each step Δt\Delta tΔt, the underlying stock price SSS can only move to one of two possible values:

  • Upward to S⋅uS \cdot uS⋅u (with risk-neutral probability ppp)
  • Downward to S⋅dS \cdot dS⋅d (with risk-neutral probability 1−p1 - p1−p)

The breakthrough of the binomial model is that it does not require knowing the actual expected drift or return of the stock. Because a delta-hedged portfolio of stock and options is risk-free over each infinitesimal interval, the option can be priced by discounting its expected payoff using risk-neutral probabilities (ppp) at the risk-free rate (rrr).

Unlike the continuous Black-Scholes-Merton formula—which can only price European options—the binomial tree works backwards through time (backward induction) from terminal expiration back to time zero. At every single intermediate node, the model checks whether the intrinsic early-exercise value exceeds the continuation value: Option Value at Node=max⁡(Immediate Exercise Payoff, e−rΔt[pfu+(1−p)fd])\text{Option Value at Node} = \max\left( \text{Immediate Exercise Payoff}, \ e^{-r \Delta t} [p f_u + (1 - p) f_d] \right)Option Value at Node=max(Immediate Exercise Payoff, e−rΔt[pfu​+(1−p)fd​])

This makes the binomial tree the industry standard for pricing American options, employee stock option grants, callable bonds, and real investment options with staged management flexibility.

📝 Notes for Future-Me

As the number of time steps NNN increases toward infinity (N→∞N \to \inftyN→∞), the discrete binomial tree converges asymptotically to the continuous Black-Scholes-Merton formula. However, for American put options, early exercise is optimal when the option is deep in-the-money and the time value of money on receiving the strike cash today exceeds the probability of further stock declines.

Always look for the "Early Exercise Frontier": if you are pricing executive stock options or private company warrants that allow pre-vest exercise, a simple Black-Scholes formula will undervalue American puts and overvalue employee options subject to early forfeiture. Use a 50-step binomial lattice for production valuation.
