Put-Call Parity and Synthetic Equivalence

Primary: John C. Hull Ch. 11 · also: Sheldon Natenberg Ch. 3
Intuition

Put-Call Parity is one of the most elegant and fundamental equilibrium laws in derivatives finance, proven by Hans Stoll in 1969. It establishes a static mathematical equality between the prices of European call options, European put options, the underlying stock, and a risk-free zero-coupon bond sharing the identical strike price (KKK) and expiration date (TTT).

The intuition rests on Portfolio Equivalence:

  • Portfolio A (Fiduciary Call): Buy one European Call (ccc) and invest the present value of the strike (Ke−rTK e^{-rT}Ke−rT) in a risk-free bond.
  • Portfolio B (Protective Put): Buy one share of underlying stock (SSS) and buy one European Put (ppp).

At expiration (TTT):

  • If ST>KS_T > KST​>K: Portfolio A call pays ST−KS_T - KST​−K and the bond matures at KKK, giving total payoff STS_TST​. Portfolio B put expires worthless, leaving the stock worth STS_TST​.
  • If ST≤KS_T \le KST​≤K: Portfolio A call expires worthless, and the bond matures at KKK. Portfolio B put exercises, allowing the investor to sell the stock for KKK.

In every possible future state of the world, both portfolios have the exact same terminal payoff: max⁡(ST,K)\max(S_T, K)max(ST​,K). Under the Law of One Price, they must cost the exact same amount today: c+Ke−rT=p+S0c + K e^{-rT} = p + S_0c+Ke−rT=p+S0​

This relationship allows traders to construct synthetic positions: any asset in the formula can be replicated synthetically using the other three.

Variable Glossary

  • ccc: European Call option price today.
  • ppp: European Put option price today.
  • S0S_0S0​: Current spot market price of the underlying asset.
  • KKK: Strike price common to both options.
  • rrr: Continuously compounded risk-free interest rate.
  • TTT: Time to expiration in years.
  • Ke−rTK e^{-rT}Ke−rT: Present value of the strike price payable at expiration.

When to Use / When Not to Use

  • When to use: Option market making, checking options pricing integrity, creating synthetic short stock when borrowing shares is impossible or expensive ("hard-to-borrow" rebate fees), and conversions/reversals arbitrage.
  • When not to use / Caveats (American Options): Strict put-call parity equality ONLY holds for European-style options. For American options (which can be exercised prior to expiration), early exercise rights on puts (or calls on dividend-paying stocks) break the strict equality, turning parity into inequality bounds: S0−K≤C−P≤S0−Ke−rTS_0 - K \le C - P \le S_0 - K e^{-rT}S0​−K≤C−P≤S0​−Ke−rT
📝 Notes for Future-Me

Synthetic equivalence is the ultimate weapon against execution friction. If you need to short a stock that has a brutal 15%15\%15% borrow fee, do not short the physical stock. Synthetically create the short by buying an at-the-money Put and selling an at-the-money Call (Ssynth=c−pS_{\text{synth}} = c - pSsynth​=c−p). The synthetic short gives you identical delta exposure and dollar-for-dollar downside participation without borrowing shares.
