Value at Risk (VaR)

Primary: John C. Hull Ch. 22 · also: Zvi Bodie Ch. 5
Intuition

Value at Risk (VaR) answers a concrete question for risk managers and regulators: What is the maximum dollar loss expected over a given time horizon at a specified confidence level? Rather than expressing risk as an abstract standard deviation (σ\sigmaσ), VaR condenses multi-asset portfolio risk into a single currency metric that senior leadership can understand.

Formula

VaR(1−α),T=Π0×(−μΔt+zα σΔt)\text{VaR}_{(1-\alpha), T} = \Pi_0 \times \left( - \mu \Delta t + z_{\alpha} \, \sigma \sqrt{\Delta t} \right)VaR(1−α),T​=Π0​×(−μΔt+zα​σΔt​)

Variable Glossary

  • Π0\Pi_0Π0​: Portfolio current market value.
  • α\alphaα: Significance level (e.g., 1%1\%1%, implying 99%99\%99% confidence).
  • zαz_{\alpha}zα​: Critical value from the standard normal distribution (z0.05≈1.645z_{0.05} \approx 1.645z0.05​≈1.645, z0.01≈2.326z_{0.01} \approx 2.326z0.01​≈2.326).
  • σ\sigmaσ: Annualized portfolio volatility.
  • Δt\Delta tΔt: Holding period in years (TTT).
  • μ\muμ: Expected return (frequently assumed to be zero over daily intervals).

Worked Numerical Example

A trading desk holds a $10,000,000 equity portfolio with annual volatility σ=20%\sigma = 20\%σ=20%. What is the 1-day 99% VaR assuming 252 trading days per year?

  • 1-Day Volatility: σ1d=0.20×1252≈1.2599%\sigma_{1\text{d}} = 0.20 \times \sqrt{\frac{1}{252}} \approx 1.2599\%σ1d​=0.20×2521​​≈1.2599%
  • 99% Normal Quantile: z=2.326z = 2.326z=2.326
  • 1-Day 99% VaR: $10,000,000×(2.326×0.012599)=$293,053\$10,000,000 \times (2.326 \times 0.012599) = \$293,053$10,000,000×(2.326×0.012599)=$293,053

There is only a 1% chance that the desk loses more than $293,053 on any given day under normal conditions.

When to Use / When Not to Use

  • When to use: Regulatory capital requirements (Basel accords), daily limit-setting, and risk monitoring.
  • When not to use / Caveats: VaR is completely blind to tail shape beyond the cutoff percentile! It tells you if you lose more than $293k, but not how much (could be $300k or $5M). For tail severity, use Expected Shortfall (CVaR).
📝 Notes for Future-Me

David Li's Gaussian copula and standard parametric VaR failed in 2008 because correlations spike toward 1.0 during liquidity crises and real-world returns have fat tails. Always pair VaR with historical stress testing.
