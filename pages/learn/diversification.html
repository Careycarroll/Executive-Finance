Diversification and Portfolio Risk

Primary: Zvi Bodie Ch. 6, 7 · also: Gabriel Hawawini Ch. 12
Intuition

Diversification has famously been called "the only free lunch in finance" (Harry Markowitz, 1952). By combining assets whose returns do not move in lockstep—meaning their correlation coefficient ρ\rhoρ is strictly less than +1.0+1.0+1.0—an investor or corporate enterprise can reduce overall portfolio variance below the weighted average variance of the individual assets, without sacrificing expected return.

In an NNN-asset portfolio, as NNN grows large, the contribution of individual asset variances shrinks toward zero, and the portfolio's total risk converges to the average pairwise covariance among all assets. Non-systematic (idiosyncratic) risks cancel out across unrelated operations, leaving only systematic market risk.

Executives encounter diversification in two domains:

  1. Treasury & Investment Portfolios: Allocating capital across uncorrelated asset classes (equities, bonds, real assets, cash reserves) to immunize against macroeconomic shocks.
  2. Corporate Conglomerate Strategy: Acquiring unrelated businesses to "smooth earnings." Unlike market investors who diversify costlessly through index funds, corporate diversification often incurs conglomerate discounts due to managerial coordination friction and agency costs unless genuine operational synergies exist.

📝 Notes for Future-Me

Beware the "Correlation 1 Trap" during market panics: during liquidity crises (e.g., 2008 Lehman collapse, March 2020 COVID shock), correlations across seemingly unrelated asset classes converge rapidly toward +1.0+1.0+1.0 as investors liquidate whatever is liquid to meet margin calls and debt covenants. The only true diversifiers in systemic crises are sovereign debt of reserve-currency issuers (US Treasuries), cash, long volatility hedges, or deep negative-correlation contracts.

Also keep corporate boundaries clear: shareholders do not need your company to buy unrelated businesses to diversify their portfolios—they can buy shares of both companies themselves for a $0 commission without paying a 30% control premium! Corporate diversification is only value-accretive when it generates balance-sheet debt capacity synergies, operational tax shields, or supply-chain resilience that markets cannot replicate frictionlessly.
