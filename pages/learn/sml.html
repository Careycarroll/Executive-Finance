Security Market Line and Alpha

Primary: Zvi Bodie Ch. 9 · also: Gabriel Hawawini Ch. 12
Intuition

The Security Market Line (SML) is the graphical representation of the Capital Asset Pricing Model (CAPM). While the Capital Market Line (CML) plots expected return against total risk (σ\sigmaσ) for complete portfolios, the SML plots expected return against systematic risk (β\betaβ) for individual securities, portfolios, or corporate business units.

In equilibrium, all correctly priced securities must lie directly on the SML.

When a security's expected return diverges from its CAPM fair required return, it generates Jensen’s Alpha (α\alphaα):

  • Positive Alpha (α>0\alpha > 0α>0): The asset plots above the SML. It generates an expected return higher than warranted by its systematic risk. The asset is underpriced (bargain).
  • Negative Alpha (α<0\alpha < 0α<0): The asset plots below the SML. It offers inadequate expected return for the risk incurred. The asset is overpriced.

Alpha is the holy grail of active investment management: generating risk-adjusted excess returns that cannot be explained by passive factor exposure.

When to Use / When Not to Use

  • When to use: Evaluating active fund manager performance, selecting individual equities for active portfolios, pricing corporate project hurdles, and identifying market mispricings.
  • When not to use / Caveats: Be careful with historical backward-looking alpha. In statistical regressions, positive alpha is often indistinguishable from statistical luck (sampling noise). True persistent alpha requires a ttt-statistic >2.0> 2.0>2.0 over at least 5 to 7 years of data.
📝 Notes for Future-Me

"Alpha" is the most abused term in financial sales pitches. When a hedge fund or asset manager pitches you a "market-beating 12% return," look at their beta first. If the market returned 15% and their beta was 1.4, their required return was 4%+1.4×11%=19.4%4\% + 1.4 \times 11\% = 19.4\%4%+1.4×11%=19.4%. That manager didn't generate positive alpha—they generated negative −7.4%-7.4\%−7.4% alpha while taking massive leveraged risk! Always strip out the SML beta return before paying any manager an incentive performance fee.
