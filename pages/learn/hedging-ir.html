Interest Rate Risk & Duration Gaps

Primary: Gabriel Hawawini Ch. 14 · also: John C. Hull Ch. 7
Intuition

Enterprises and financial institutions face structural interest rate risk across two interrelated domains:

  1. Net Interest Income Sensitivity (Earnings at Risk): The short-term impact of rate swings on annual accounting net interest margin, governed by the Repricing Gap: $$\text{GAP} = \text{Rate-Sensitive Assets (RSA)} - \text{Rate-Sensitive Liabilities (RSL)}$$ When $\text{GAP} > 0$ (asset-sensitive), rising interest rates expand net interest income. When $\text{GAP} < 0$ (liability-sensitive), rising interest rates compress margins.
  2. Economic Value of Equity (EVE Sensitivity): The long-term impact of rate swings on the total net worth of the institution, governed by the Duration Gap ($DG$): $$\text{Duration Gap} = D_A - \left(\frac{L}{A}\right) \cdot D_L$$

If an institution's assets have longer duration than its liabilities ($DG > 0$), a sudden spike in interest rates destroys asset values faster than liabilities decline, creating a severe drain on equity capital.

Treasury teams manage interest rate risk using three primary tools:

  • Interest Rate Swaps (IRS): Swapping floating-rate bank credit facility interest for fixed-rate payments.
  • Interest Rate Caps & Collars: Purchasing an interest rate cap sets a ceiling on maximum floating borrowing costs while preserving downside savings if rates fall.
  • Eurodollar / SOFR Futures: Adjusting portfolio duration dynamically using exchange-traded interest rate contracts.

Formula

\Delta \text{EVE} \approx -\left[ D_A - \left(\frac{L}{A}\right) \cdot D_L \right] \cdot A \cdot \Delta y = -DG \cdot A \cdot \Delta y $$\text{Number of Swap Contracts to Immunize: } N^* = \frac{DG \cdot A}{D_{\text{swap}} \cdot V_{\text{swap}}}$$ $$\text{Annual Net Interest Income Impact: } \Delta \text{NII} = \text{GAP} \cdot \Delta y

Variable Glossary

  • \Delta \text{EVE}: Change in Economic Value of Equity (market value of net worth).
  • DG: Duration Gap ($D_A - \frac{L}{A} D_L$).
  • D_A / D_L: Modified duration of total assets and total liabilities.
  • A / L: Market value of total assets and total liabilities.
  • \Delta y: Parallel interest rate shift across the yield curve.
  • \text{NII}: Net Interest Income ($\text{Interest Revenues} - \text{Interest Expenses}$).
  • \text{GAP}: Dollar volume of assets maturing/repricing within 1 year minus liabilities repricing within 1 year.

Worked Numerical Example

A regional financial institution reports the following balance sheet metrics:

  • Total Assets ($A$): $$1{,}000{,}000{,}000$ with asset duration $D_A = 5.0\text{ years}$
  • Total Liabilities ($L$): $$900{,}000{,}000$ with liability duration $D_L = 1.5\text{ years}$
  • Total Equity Capital: $$100{,}000{,}000$ (Equity-to-Assets ratio $= 10%$)
  • Interest Rate Shock: Central bank raises benchmark rates by $+200\text{ bps}$ ($\Delta y = +0.02$).

Step 1: Calculate the Duration Gap ($DG$) $$DG = D_A - \left(\frac{L}{A}\right) D_L = 5.0 - \left(\frac{$900\text{M}}{$1{,}000\text{M}}\right) \times 1.5 = 5.0 - (0.90 \times 1.5) = 5.0 - 1.35 = +3.65\text{ years}$$

Step 2: Calculate Economic Value of Equity Impact $$\Delta \text{EVE} \approx -DG \cdot A \cdot \Delta y = -3.65 \times $1{,}000{,}000{,}000 \times (+0.02) = -$73{,}000{,}000$$

Catastrophic Balance Sheet Evaluation: A $+200\text{ bps}$ rate hike wipes out $$73,000,000$ of the bank’s $$100,000,000$ equity capital ($73%$ of net worth eliminated overnight!). To immunize the balance sheet ($DG \to 0$), the treasurer must either shorten asset duration (selling long bonds, originating floating loans) or enter a pay-fixed / receive-floating interest rate swap portfolio with notional value of $$73\text{M} / \Delta y$.

When to Use / When Not to Use

  • When to use: Corporate debt issuance structuring (floating vs. fixed mix), bank asset-liability committee (ALCO) oversight, commercial real estate mortgage debt hedging, and pension liability matching.
  • When not to use / Caveats: Duration gaps assume parallel shifts across the yield curve. If the short end of the curve spikes by 300 bps while the 10-year yield stays flat (curve inversion), standard duration gap models fail to predict earnings compression. Multi-bucket key-rate duration analysis is required.
📝 Notes for Future-Me

"The cost of hedging is an insurance premium; the cost of unhedged leverage is bankruptcy." When corporate borrowing rates are at cyclical lows (e.g., 2%–3%), CEOs are tempted to stay on floating bank revolvers because floating rates are 50 bps cheaper than 10-year fixed bonds. This is the ultimate penny-wise, pound-foolish mistake: you save 50 bps in good times, and when rates spike to 7%, your entire operating cash flow is consumed by interest debt service! Always lock in long-term fixed rates or buy interest rate caps when rates are historically low.
