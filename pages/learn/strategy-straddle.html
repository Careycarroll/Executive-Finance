Long Straddle Strategy

Primary: Sheldon Natenberg Ch. 12 · also: John C. Hull Ch. 12
Intuition

The Long Straddle is the quintessential pure volatility strategy in derivatives trading. It consists of buying an At-the-Money (ATM) Call option and simultaneously buying an At-the-Money Put option with the exact same strike price (KKK) and expiration date (TTT).

The trade is directionally neutral (Delta ≈0\approx 0≈0): the trader does not care whether the stock moves up or down. Instead, the straddle is a pure bet on magnitude of movement (large realized volatility):

  • If the stock makes a massive move upward, the call gains unlimited profit while the put loss is capped at its purchase price.
  • If the stock collapses downward, the put gains substantial profit while the call loss is capped at its purchase price.
  • The Enemy of the Straddle is Stagnation: If the stock stays pinned near the strike price KKK through expiration, both options lose all time value, resulting in a 100%100\%100% total loss of the net premium paid.

The combined premium paid defines two distinct Breakeven Points: the stock must move outside of this breakeven corridor by expiration for the trade to be profitable.

Whenever you consider buying a straddle ahead of an earnings release or FDA announcement:

  1. Calculate the breakeven move required: if the straddle costs $10.00\$10.00$10.00 on a $100.00\$100.00$100.00 stock, the stock must move by more than ±10.0%\pm 10.0\%±10.0% just to break even!
  2. Review historical earnings moves: if the stock has historically only moved an average of ±5.5%\pm 5.5\%±5.5% on earnings days over the past 8 quarters, buying the straddle at a 10%10\%10% implied move is a negative expected value proposition. The market has priced in the event; you are buying overpriced implied volatility.
