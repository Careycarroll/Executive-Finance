⚠️ Common Misconceptions
  • Misconception 1: "If my American call option on a non-dividend tech stock has doubled in value, I should exercise it to lock in profits."
    Reality: Never exercise an in-the-money call on a non-dividend stock before expiration—sell the call option in the market instead! If a $100 strike call is trading at $25 while the stock is at $122, exercising gives you $22 of equity value and throws $3.00 of extrinsic time value straight into the trash.
  • Misconception 2: "High interest rates reduce the incentive to exercise American puts early."
    Reality: High interest rates increase the incentive to exercise deep-in-the-money American puts early! Exercising a put hands you cash (KKK) today; when risk-free rates are 5%+5\%+5%+, getting that cash immediately to invest in Treasury bills is highly valuable.
  • Misconception 3: "Put-Call Parity holds as an exact equality for American options."
    Reality: Exact Put-Call Parity (C+Ke−rT=P+S0C + K e^{-rT} = P + S_0C+Ke−rT=P+S0​) only holds for European options. For American options, it is replaced by inequality bounds: S0−K≤CAmer−PAmer≤S0−Ke−rTS_0 - K \le C_{\text{Amer}} - P_{\text{Amer}} \le S_0 - K e^{-rT}S0​−K≤CAmer​−PAmer​≤S0​−Ke−rT.
📝 Notes for Future-Me

If you are trading Covered Calls or Vertical Call Spreads on dividend-paying equities, watch out for Dividend Assignment Risk: If your short call is in-the-money the afternoon before the stock goes ex-dividend, and the remaining extrinsic time value of the corresponding put is less than the upcoming cash dividend (Pextrinsic<DP_{\text{extrinsic}} < DPextrinsic​<D), institutional arbitrageurs will automatically exercise your short call overnight to capture the dividend! Close or roll in-the-money short calls the day before the ex-dividend date to avoid unexpected short-stock dividend liabilities.
