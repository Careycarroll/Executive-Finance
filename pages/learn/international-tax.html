Arm's-Length Transfer Pricing (IRC §482) Intercompany goods, services, royalties, and loans. Requires benchmarking intercompany margins against independent comparables (CUP, Cost-Plus, Resale Price, or CPM methods). Prevents arbitrary invoice shifting; requires Contemporaneous Transfer Pricing Studies to avoid 20%−40%20\%-40\%20%−40% IRS penalties.

⚠️ Common Misconceptions
  • Misconception 1: "A US company can eliminate its US taxes simply by transferring its patents to a Cayman Islands subsidiary for $1."
    Reality: Under IRC Section 367(d) and the "Commensurate with Income" standard, transferring valuable IP offshore triggers an immediate exit tax or forces the US parent to impute an annual royalty stream equal to the actual future economic profits generated by that IP! Furthermore, Subpart F and GILTI immediately tax offshore passive and low-taxed intangible income.
  • Misconception 2: "Foreign Tax Credits (FTCs) give a dollar-for-dollar refund for all foreign taxes paid."
    Reality: Foreign Tax Credits are strictly capped at the US tax rate that would have been owed on that foreign income (FTC Limit=US Tax×Foreign Taxable IncomeTotal Taxable Income\text{FTC Limit} = \text{US Tax} \times \frac{\text{Foreign Taxable Income}}{\text{Total Taxable Income}}FTC Limit=US Tax×Total Taxable IncomeForeign Taxable Income​), segregated into separate statutory baskets (GILTI basket, Passive basket, General basket). Excess foreign credits paid in high-tax countries (30%30\%30%) cannot be used to offset US taxes on domestic income.
📝 Notes for Future-Me

When expanding internationally or managing cross-border supply chains:

  1. Pair FDII vs. GILTI Before Moving IP: Do the math before spending millions in legal fees to migrate Intellectual Property offshore. With the US FDII deduction offering a 13.125%13.125\%13.125% tax rate on export income kept inside the US and OECD Pillar Two imposing a 15%15\%15% minimum tax offshore, keeping IP onshore in Delaware is now frequently more tax-efficient and vastly simpler than offshore tax structures!
  2. Secure an Advance Pricing Agreement (APA): For large, recurring intercompany royalty or manufacturing flows, negotiate a bilateral Advance Pricing Agreement directly with the IRS and the foreign tax authority upfront to lock in guaranteed transfer pricing methodology and eliminate double-taxation audit risk.
