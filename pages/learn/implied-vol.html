Implied vs. Realized Volatility

Primary: Sheldon Natenberg Ch. 4 · also: Euan Sinclair Ch. 2
Intuition

Volatility is the primary driver of option pricing and risk management. However, financial markets operate on two radically different manifestations of volatility:

  1. Realized Volatility (σrealized\sigma_{\text{realized}}σrealized​ / Historical Volatility): The actual, backward-looking statistical standard deviation of asset price returns observed over a historical window (e.g., trailing 30, 60, or 252 trading days). It measures what actually happened in the physical world.
  2. Implied Volatility (σimplied\sigma_{\text{implied}}σimplied​ / IV): The forward-looking market consensus expectation of future volatility, derived by inverting the Black-Scholes formula from currently observed market option prices. It represents the price of volatility insurance demanded by market makers today.

The persistent spread between the two creates the Volatility Risk Premium (VRP): VRP=σimplied−σrealized\text{VRP} = \sigma_{\text{implied}} - \sigma_{\text{realized}}VRP=σimplied​−σrealized​

Empirical market data shows that across broad equity indices (S&P 500 VIX), implied volatility trades higher than subsequent realized volatility approximately 80%−85%80\%-85\%80%−85% of the time! Option buyers systematically pay an insurance risk premium to protect against catastrophic tail risk, creating an enduring structural edge for disciplined options sellers.

  • 30-day Realized Volatility (σrealized\sigma_{\text{realized}}σrealized​): 20.0%20.0\%20.0% per annum.
  • 30-day At-The-Money Option Implied Volatility (σIV\sigma_{\text{IV}}σIV​): 32.0%32.0\%32.0% per annum.

Strategic Takeaway: Buying 30-day put options at a 32%32\%32% IV to hedge a stock that is moving only 1.25%1.25\%1.25% per day means you are overpaying by nearly 60%60\%60% for volatility protection. Instead of buying naked puts, the manager should execute a collar (buy put funded by selling a call) or sell an out-of-the-money credit spread to capture the elevated VRP.

When to Use / When Not to Use

  • When to use: Systematic options selling strategies, market making, evaluating whether options are "rich" or "cheap" relative to history via IV Rank (IVR) and IV Percentile (IVP), and timing hedge entries.
  • When not to use / Caveats: Do not assume that high IV is always overpriced. When a genuine geopolitical or credit crisis erupts (e.g., 2008 Lehman collapse, 2020 pandemic), realized volatility explodes from 15% to 80% in days, completely overwhelming implied volatility and wiping out short-volatility funds.
