Position Sizing & The Kelly Criterion

Primary: Euan Sinclair Ch. 5 · also: Zvi Bodie Ch. 6
Intuition

In quantitative trading and speculative capital allocation, having a positive mathematical edge (EV>0\text{EV} > 0EV>0) is necessary, but wholly insufficient to prevent catastrophic bankruptcy. If a trader or enterprise bets too aggressively on favorable bets, sequential streaks of bad variance will trigger the Gambler's Ruin (irreversible capital drawdown). Conversely, betting too little leaves compounding wealth on the table.

John L. Kelly Jr. (Bell Labs, 1956) solved this by deriving the Kelly Criterion: the exact fraction of total bankroll or capital that maximizes the long-term expected geometric growth rate of wealth (E[ln⁡(W∞)]\mathbb{E}[\ln(W_{\infty})]E[ln(W∞​)]).

The core insight of the Kelly Criterion is that geometric compounding penalizes volatility exponentially:

  • Betting less than the Kelly fraction (f<f∗f < f^*f<f∗) produces sub-optimal wealth growth, but lowers drawdown volatility.
  • Betting exactly full Kelly (f=f∗f = f^*f=f∗) maximizes compounding growth, but incurs extreme, stomach-churning drawdowns (up to 50%−80%50\%-80\%50%−80% peak-to-trough drawdowns).
  • Betting more than full Kelly (f>f∗f > f^*f>f∗) is catastrophic: you take more risk for lower expected compounding growth, eventually converging to guaranteed mathematical ruin (W→0W \to 0W→0).

Professional institutional traders and hedge funds universally employ Fractional Kelly (typically Half-Kelly or Quarter-Kelly, f∗/2f^* / 2f∗/2 or f∗/4f^* / 4f∗/4) to capture 75%−90%75\%-90\%75%−90% of optimal growth while slashing drawdown severity by half.

When to Use / When Not to Use

  • When to use: Sizing algorithmic trading bets, allocating corporate venture capital across early-stage seed projects, setting position limits for credit default desks, and deciding capital commitments in binary M&A auctions.
  • When not to use / Caveats: NEVER use Full Kelly when win probability ppp is based on short-term backtests! Backtests suffer from overfitting and survivorship bias. Overestimating your edge by even 20%20\%20% when using Full Kelly pushes your allocation into the negative-compounding death zone (f>2f∗f > 2 f^*f>2f∗), guaranteeing account destruction.
📝 Notes for Future-Me

"Surviving variance is the prerequisite to compounding." The most brilliant mathematical edge in the world is useless if you get margin-called on step 14 before the law of large numbers kicks in. Always default to Quarter-Kelly or Half-Kelly. If your model tells you to put 30% of your fund into a trade, scale it back to 7.5%. You will sacrifice only a tiny fraction of peak theoretical upside while buying complete structural immunity against black swan wipeouts.
