⚠️ Common Misconceptions
  • Misconception 1: "Gamma scalping is free money as long as the stock moves back and forth."
    Reality: Gamma scalping only profits if Realized Volatility exceeds the Implied Volatility you paid when buying the options (σrealized>σimplied\sigma_{\text{realized}} > \sigma_{\text{implied}}σrealized​>σimplied​). If you buy options at 40%40\%40% IV (2.5%2.5\%2.5%/day breakeven move) and the stock chops back and forth by only 1.2%1.2\%1.2%/day, your scalping gains will recover less than a quarter of your daily Theta bleed!
  • Misconception 2: "Re-hedging Delta every 5 minutes maximizes Gamma scalping profits."
    Reality: In the presence of bid-ask spreads and commissions, hedging too frequently bleeds capital on transaction friction and whipsaws. Professional volatility traders hedge on Delta-band thresholds (e.g., only rebalance when net portfolio Delta exceeds ±0.15Δ\pm 0.15\Delta±0.15Δ or when the stock moves by 1.0×1.0\times1.0× the daily implied move), not on fixed clock intervals.
📝 Notes for Future-Me

Never initiate a Long Gamma scalping book unless two conditions hold simultaneously:

  1. Valuation Edge: Implied Volatility is trading at a verified discount to forecasted Realized Volatility (σIV<σ^RV\sigma_{\text{IV}} < \hat{\sigma}_{\text{RV}}σIV​<σ^RV​).
  2. Low Bid-Ask Friction: The underlying asset is an ultra-liquid futures contract or ETF (e.g., ES, NQ, SPY, QQQ) where delta-hedging execution costs less than 1 basis point per trade. If the stock gaps strongly in one direction without mean-reverting, remember that letting your Delta run slightly before hedging captures convex trend profits, whereas hedging too early leaves you under-participating in a breakout.
