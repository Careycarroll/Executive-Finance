Discounted Cash Flow Valuation (Enterprise vs Equity)

Primary: Russell Lundholm Ch. 4, 5 · also: Stephen H. Penman Ch. 4
Intuition

Discounted Cash Flow (DCF) valuation is the gold standard of fundamental intrinsic valuation. It embodies the core axiom of finance: an asset is worth the present value of the future cash flows it will generate for its owners, discounted at a rate reflecting the risk and timing of those cash flows.

Practitioners employ two primary DCF architectures:

  1. The Enterprise Approach (Unlevered DCF): Forecasts Free Cash Flow to the Firm (FCFF) over an explicit forecast horizon plus a Terminal Value (TV) representing perpetual post-forecast operations. FCFF is discounted at the Weighted Average Cost of Capital (WACC) to determine Enterprise Value (EV). Equity value is derived by adding non-operating cash and subtracting net debt (Equity Value=EV+Cash−Debt\text{Equity Value} = \text{EV} + \text{Cash} - \text{Debt}Equity Value=EV+Cash−Debt).
  2. The Equity Approach (Levered DCF): Forecasts Free Cash Flow to Equity (FCFE) directly, discounted at the Cost of Equity (rer_ere​) to arrive directly at Equity Value.

The Enterprise approach is universally preferred for non-financial corporations because it isolates the core operating performance of the business from arbitrary leverage and capital structure decisions.

📝 Notes for Future-Me

DCF models suffer from the "Terminal Value Domination Problem": in a standard 5-year DCF, the Terminal Value typically represents 65%−85%65\%-85\%65%−85% of the total calculated Enterprise Value! This means the 40-tab financial model detailing quarterly revenues, gross margins, and headcount across the 5-year forecast horizon is driving less than a third of the total valuation; the remaining two-thirds hangs entirely on two subjective assumptions: the exit WACC and the perpetuity growth rate ggg.

Always run a two-variable sensitivity table crossing WACC against ggg (or against exit EV/EBITDA multiple). If the valuation drops by 35% when WACC rises from 8.5% to 9.5% or when ggg falls from 3% to 2%, your model is not providing certainty—it is mapping vulnerability.
