Working Capital Management & Cash Conversion Cycle

Primary: Gabriel Hawawini Ch. 5, 6 · also: Peter D. Easton Ch. 4
Intuition

Working capital management governs the operational circulatory system of an enterprise. It balances two competing executive priorities: liquidity (ensuring the business never defaults on short-term payroll or supplier obligations) and profitability (minimizing the amount of expensive capital trapped idly in accounts receivable, raw materials, and unsold warehouse inventory).

Hawawini & Viallet introduce a vital distinction often missed in generic accounting:

  • Working Capital Requirement (WCR / Operating Net Working Capital): The net operating capital tied up in the business cycle: Operating Current Assets (Inventories + Receivables)−Operating Current Liabilities (Payables)\text{Operating Current Assets (Inventories + Receivables)} - \text{Operating Current Liabilities (Payables)}Operating Current Assets (Inventories + Receivables)−Operating Current Liabilities (Payables).
  • Cash Conversion Cycle (CCC): The time lag (in days) between the cash outflow paid to suppliers for raw materials and the cash inflow collected from customers for finished sales.

A shorter Cash Conversion Cycle accelerates internal cash generation, boosting Free Cash Flow and reducing the need for external bank credit lines. Elite supply-chain operators (e.g., Apple, Dell, Amazon) operate with negative Cash Conversion Cycles, effectively financing their entire working capital on zero-interest credit provided by their suppliers!

Variable Glossary

  • CCC\text{CCC}CCC: Cash Conversion Cycle (net operating cash cycle in days).
  • DIO\text{DIO}DIO: Days Inventory Outstanding (average days inventory sits in stock before being sold).
  • DSO\text{DSO}DSO: Days Sales Outstanding (average days required to collect cash from customers after a credit sale; also called Average Collection Period).
  • DPO\text{DPO}DPO: Days Payables Outstanding (average days taken to pay vendors and suppliers for credit purchases).
  • COGS\text{COGS}COGS: Cost of Goods Sold.
  • WCR\text{WCR}WCR: Working Capital Requirement (Operating NWC).

When to Use / When Not to Use

  • When to use: Post-merger operational turnarounds, cash forecasting during rapid sales growth, bank credit facility sizing, and assessing supplier credit risk.
  • When not to use / Caveats: Beware of destructive CCC "optimizations." Artificially stretching DPO to 120 days by ignoring supplier payment terms destroys supplier relationships, loses cash discounts (e.g., 2/10 net 30), and risks parts delivery boycotts. Similarly, slashing inventory too aggressively induces expensive factory stockouts and missed customer deliveries.
📝 Notes for Future-Me

"Growth eats cash." The single most dangerous phase for a rapidly scaling business is a sudden doubling of sales. Because accounts receivable and inventory must be funded weeks or months before customer cash arrives, a doubling of revenue causes Working Capital Requirements (ΔWCR\Delta\text{WCR}ΔWCR) to explode. Without adequate revolving credit or cash cushions, a wildly profitable company can suffer a catastrophic liquidity crisis ("overtrading"). Always model working capital cash drains at every stage of growth projections.
