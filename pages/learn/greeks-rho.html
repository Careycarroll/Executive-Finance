⚠️ Common Misconceptions
  • Misconception 1: "Higher interest rates increase call option prices, so tech call options will profit when the Fed hikes rates."
    Reality: Remember ceteris paribus (all else equal)! While a +100 bps+100\text{ bps}+100 bps rate hike adds positive Rho to a call option if the stock price stays constant, in the real world a surprise Fed rate hike often triggers a −5%-5\%−5% drop in the underlying stock price (Delta loss)! On a 3-month option, the negative Delta impact of a stock sell-off completely overwhelms the tiny positive Rho gain.
  • Misconception 2: "Futures options (like S&P E-mini or Crude Oil options) have the same Rho sign as stock options."
    Reality: Under the Black-76 Model for options on futures contracts, because entering a futures contract requires zero upfront cash outlay (no cost of carry on the underlying), a rise in interest rates simply discounts the future option payoff more heavily! Therefore, for options on futures, both Calls and Puts have negative Rho (ρfutures call=−T⋅C<0\rho_{\text{futures call}} = -T \cdot C < 0ρfutures call​=−T⋅C<0)!
📝 Notes for Future-Me

When valuing Long-Term Employee Stock Options (ASC 718), M&A Contingent Value Rights, or 2-Year LEAPS: Never ignore Rho during interest rate regime shifts. Moving from a 0.5%0.5\%0.5% zero-rate environment to a 5.0%5.0\%5.0% risk-free rate increases the Black-Scholes value of an at-the-money 3-year Call option by over 20%20\%20% even if stock price and implied volatility do not move an inch! Always pull the exact maturity-matched US Treasury Strip yield when pricing multi-year equity derivatives.
