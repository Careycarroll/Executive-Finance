Part I Chapter 1

Financial Management and Value Creation

Before making any business decision, ask the Key Question: will this decision create value? Learn to measure value creation with NPV, read financial statements, and determine whether a firm is creating or destroying value.

Not started

Learning Objectives

  1. Understand the objective of financial management: value creation
  2. Apply the fundamental finance principle using net present value (NPV)
  3. Understand the role of financial markets as sources of capital and processors of information
  4. Understand the firm business cycle and the self-sustainable growth rate
  5. Read and interpret the balance sheet and the managerial balance sheet
  6. Read and interpret the income statement
  7. Estimate cash flows and understand the statement of cash flows
  8. Measure and understand business risk and financial risk
  9. Measure firm profitability using ROE and ROIC
  10. Determine whether a firm has created value by comparing ROIC to WACC
Tool 1 of 5

Managerial Balance Sheet Builder

The managerial balance sheet restructures the standard balance sheet to reveal how much capital is truly invested in the business and where it comes from. Working Capital Requirement (WCR) nets out operating liabilities from operating assets, showing the net investment needed to run the firm.

Balance Sheet Inputs

Current Assets

Non-Current Assets

Liabilities

Tool 2 of 5

Income Statement Analyzer

The income statement shows how sales revenue flows through expenses to produce profit for shareholders. EBIT measures operating profitability before financing costs. EAT is what belongs to shareholders. ROE and ROIC measure how efficiently the firm uses its capital.

Income Statement Inputs

Capital Base (for ROE and ROIC)

Tool 3 of 5

Cash Flow Statement Builder

Profit is not cash. The statement of cash flows reconciles accounting profit to actual cash movements by separating operating, investing, and financing activities. A firm that consistently fails to generate cash from operations is headed for trouble regardless of reported profits.

Cash Flow Inputs

Operating Activities

Investing Activities

Financing Activities

Opening Position

Tool 4 of 5

Value Creation Analyzer

A firm creates value only when its return on invested capital (ROIC) exceeds its weighted average cost of capital (WACC). The difference -- the return spread -- multiplied by invested capital gives Economic Value Added (EVA): the dollar amount of value created or destroyed in the period.

ROIC and WACC Inputs

Operating Performance

Cost of Capital

Tool 5 of 5

NPV and IRR Decision Tool

The fundamental finance principle in action. A proposal creates value only if its NPV is positive -- meaning the present value of future cash flows exceeds the initial outlay. The IRR is the discount rate that makes NPV equal to zero. Accept if IRR exceeds the cost of capital.

Investment Inputs

Expected Cash Flows (up to 5 years)
