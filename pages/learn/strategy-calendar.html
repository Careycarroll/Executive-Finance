⚠️ Common Misconceptions
  • Misconception 1: "A Long Calendar Spread always profits when implied volatility rises."
    Reality: This is only true for a parallel shift in volatility across both months! In the real world, when a sudden market panic or earnings rumor hits, front-month volatility (σ1\sigma_1σ1​) spikes 3×3\times3× harder than back-month volatility (σ2\sigma_2σ2​), driving the term structure into Backwardation. Even though your back-month option has a higher raw Vega number, a violent spike in front-month IV relative to back-month IV can cause a Long Calendar Spread to lose money! Always weight your Vega by the term-structure beta (Weighted Vega: Vw=V⋅Tbase/T\mathcal{V}_w = \mathcal{V} \cdot \sqrt{T_{\text{base}} / T}Vw​=V⋅Tbase​/T​).
  • Misconception 2: "You should buy a Long Calendar Spread right before an earnings announcement because IV is high."
    Reality: Before earnings, front-month IV is massively inflated relative to back-month IV (steep backwardation). If you buy a Calendar before earnings, you are selling elevated front-month vol (good), unless the stock makes a huge post-earnings gap outside your tent, where Gamma kills the position. Conversely, never buy a Calendar when front-month IV is elevated if you expect both months to crush equally.
📝 Notes for Future-Me

The two golden rules for trading Calendar and Diagonal Spreads:

  1. Check the Term Structure Slope First: Only buy Long Calendar Spreads when the Volatility Term Structure is in Contango or flat (σfront≤σback\sigma_{\text{front}} \le \sigma_{\text{back}}σfront​≤σback​), or when you have a specific catalyst (like an earnings date in T1T_1T1​) where front-month IV is 20+ vol points20+\text{ vol points}20+ vol points higher than T2T_2T2​ and you expect a smaller-than-implied stock move.
  2. Poor Man's Covered Call Rule: When structuring a Bullish Call Diagonal (buying a 0.80Δ0.80\Delta0.80Δ back-month call and selling a 0.30Δ0.30\Delta0.30Δ front-month call), ensure the Net Debit paid is less than the strike width (K2−K1K_2 - K_1K2​−K1​). That guarantees you cannot lose money if the stock explodes upward through your short strike!
