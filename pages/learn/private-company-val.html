###Multiplicative Discount Math (Never Add Discounts Linearly!) DLOC and DLOM are applied sequentially (multiplicatively), never added together: Combined Discount=1−[(1−DLOC)×(1−DLOM)]\text{Combined Discount} = 1 - \left[ (1 - \text{DLOC}) \times (1 - \text{DLOM}) \right]Combined Discount=1−[(1−DLOC)×(1−DLOM)] Relationship Between Control Premium (CP) and DLOC: DLOC=1−11+CP\text{Relationship Between Control Premium (CP) and DLOC: } \text{DLOC} = 1 - \frac{1}{1 + \text{CP}}Relationship Between Control Premium (CP) and DLOC: DLOC=1−1+CP1​ (Example: A 25%25\%25% Control Premium implies a DLOC=1−11.25=20.0%\text{DLOC} = 1 - \frac{1}{1.25} = 20.0\%DLOC=1−1.251​=20.0%).

⚠️ Common Misconceptions
  • Misconception 1: "If DLOC is 20% and DLOM is 30%, the total valuation discount is 50%."
    Reality: Discounts apply sequentially to the remaining value base: 1−(1−0.20)(1−0.30)=1−(0.80×0.70)=1−0.56=44.0%1 - (1 - 0.20)(1 - 0.30) = 1 - (0.80 \times 0.70) = 1 - 0.56 = 44.0\%1−(1−0.20)(1−0.30)=1−(0.80×0.70)=1−0.56=44.0%, not 50%50\%50%.
  • Misconception 2: "You should apply a Control Premium to every DCF valuation when buying a company."
    Reality: Look at the cash flows you modeled in the DCF! If your DCF model already includes optimized margins, post-deal synergies, and optimal capital structure, your DCF output is already at Level 1 / Level 2 (Control Value)! Slapping an additional 30% Control Premium on top of an optimized DCF double-counts control and causes massive overpayment.
  • Misconception 3: "Private company financials can be used directly without owner normalization."
    Reality: Private business owners routinely run personal expenses (vehicles, travel, family salaries, above-market rent on owner-owned real estate) through the income statement to minimize taxes, or pay themselves below-market salaries. Always normalize private EBITDA for Fair Market Executive Compensation and arm's-length rent before applying any valuation model.
📝 Notes for Future-Me

How to estimate the Discount for Lack of Marketability (DLOM) rigorously without guessing:

  1. Restricted Stock Studies / Pre-IPO Studies: Empirical price discounts observed on restricted Rule 144 stock placements compared to freely traded shares (15%−30%15\%-30\%15%−30%).
  2. Option-Pricing DLOM Models (Chaffe / Finnerty / Longstaff): Model the cost of illiquidity as the value of an At-the-Money Put Option (or Asian lookback put) with time to expiration equal to the expected years until a liquidity event (TTT) and volatility equal to peer stock volatility (σ\sigmaσ). Higher volatility and longer lock-up horizons mathematically justify a larger DLOM.
