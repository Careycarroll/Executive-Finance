# Free Cash Flow (FCFF vs FCFE)

## Intuition
Accounting earnings (Net Income, EBITDA) are opinions; cash flow is fact. Free Cash Flow (FCF) measures the actual cash generated by a business's operations that remains available for distribution to capital providers after funding all required operating expenses, working capital expansions, and capital expenditures (CapEx) needed to maintain and grow the asset base.

Executive finance distinguishes two fundamental tiers of Free Cash Flow:
1. **Free Cash Flow to the Firm (FCFF / Unlevered Free Cash Flow):** Cash available to **all capital providers** (both debt holders and equity shareholders). It is computed before any interest expenses or debt principal repayments, reflecting the operating productivity of the enterprise independent of its financing capital structure. FCFF is discounted at the **WACC** to determine total **Enterprise Value**.
2. **Free Cash Flow to Equity (FCFE / Levered Free Cash Flow):** Cash available strictly to **common shareholders** after paying operating taxes, reinvesting in the business, and servicing net debt (interest payments and net debt repayments/borrowings). FCFE is discounted at the **Cost of Equity ($r_e$)** to determine **Equity Value**.

## Formula
$$\text{FCFF} = \text{NOPAT} + \text{D\&A} - \Delta\text{NWC} - \text{CapEx} = \text{EBIT}(1 - t) + \text{D\&A} - \Delta\text{NWC} - \text{CapEx}$$

$$\text{FCFE} = \text{FCFF} - \text{Interest}(1 - t) + \text{Net Borrowing} = \text{Net Income} + \text{D\&A} - \Delta\text{NWC} - \text{CapEx} + \text{Net Debt Issued}$$

## Variable Glossary
- **$FCFF$**: Free Cash Flow to the Firm (Unlevered FCF).
- **$FCFE$**: Free Cash Flow to Equity (Levered FCF).
- **NOPAT**: Net Operating Profit After Tax ($= \text{EBIT} \times (1 - t)$).
- **$EBIT$**: Earnings Before Interest and Taxes (operating income).
- **$t$**: Marginal effective corporate tax rate.
- **D&A**: Non-cash Depreciation and Amortization expenses added back.
- **$\Delta NWC$**: Change in Non-Cash Working Capital ($(\text{Current Assets} - \text{Cash}) - (\text{Current Liabilities} - \text{Short-Term Debt})$).
- **CapEx**: Capital expenditures on property, plant, equipment, and capitalized software.
- **Net Borrowing**: New debt principal issued minus debt principal repaid during the period.

## Worked Example
A high-growth logistics technology firm reports the following annual financial results:
- **EBIT:** $\$80{,}000{,}000$
- **Marginal Tax Rate ($t$):** $25\%$
- **Depreciation & Amortization:** $\$15{,}000{,}000$
- **Capital Expenditures:** $\$22{,}000{,}000$
- **Working Capital:** Operating Current Assets grew by $\$14{,}000{,}000$, while Operating Current Liabilities grew by $\$6{,}000{,}000$ ($\Delta\text{NWC} = +\$8{,}000{,}000$).
- **Interest Expense:** $\$12{,}000{,}000$ (after-tax interest $= \$12\text{M} \times (1 - 0.25) = \$9{,}000{,}000$).
- **Net Debt Activity:** Issued $\$10{,}000{,}000$ of term notes and repaid $\$4{,}000{,}000$ of credit line ($\text{Net Borrowing} = +\$6{,}000{,}000$).

**Step 1: Calculate FCFF (Unlevered)**
$$\text{NOPAT} = \$80{,}000{,}000 \times (1 - 0.25) = \$60{,}000{,}000$$
$$\text{FCFF} = \$60{,}000{,}000 + \$15{,}000{,}000 - \$8{,}000{,}000 - \$22{,}000{,}000 = \$45{,}000{,}000$$
The enterprise generated $\$45{,}000{,}000$ of pure operating cash for all investors.

**Step 2: Calculate FCFE (Levered)**
$$\text{FCFE} = \text{FCFF} - \text{Interest}(1 - t) + \text{Net Borrowing}$$
$$\text{FCFE} = \$45{,}000{,}000 - \$9{,}000{,}000 + \$6{,}000{,}000 = \$42{,}000{,}000$$
Common equity holders can safely receive up to $\$42{,}000{,}000$ in cash dividends or share repurchases without depleting operating liquidity or default covenants.

## When to Use
- **When to use FCFF:** Standard corporate valuation (DCF enterprise value), multi-division capital budgeting, cross-border M&A target screening where target capital structures will be completely re-leveraged post-acquisition.
- **When to use FCFE:** Valuing financial institutions (banks, insurers, brokerages where debt is raw inventory rather than external capital structure), or companies with stable, pre-targeted debt-to-equity ratios.
- **Caveats:** EBITDA is NOT Free Cash Flow! Warren Buffett famously quipped: *"Does management think the tooth fairy pays for capital expenditures?"* Never let an acquisition memo substitute EBITDA for FCF without subtracting mandatory Maintenance CapEx, tax cash outlays, and working capital drain.

## Notes for Future-Me
When inspecting financial projections, check the delta between **Net Income** and **FCFF**. If a company is reporting rapid revenue and accounting profit growth but persistent negative FCFF, they are either burning cash in ballooning uncollected accounts receivable ($\Delta\text{NWC}$ trap) or capitalizing regular expenses into aggressive long-term software CapEx assets. Earnings growth without free cash flow conversion is a classic precursor to emergency dilutive equity down-rounds or liquidity distress.
