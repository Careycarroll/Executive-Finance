# Cross-Currency Swaps (FX Swaps)

## Intuition
While a standard **Interest Rate Swap (IRS)** exchanges fixed vs. floating interest payments in a *single* currency without ever exchanging principal, a **Cross-Currency Swap (CCS)** exchanges both **principal and interest payments across two different currencies** (e.g., US Dollars and Euros) over a multi-year tenor (Hull Ch. 7).

Cross-currency swaps are engineered to exploit **Comparative Advantage in Global Credit Markets** and hedge long-term foreign asset balance sheets. For example, a US corporation may have brand recognition and cheap borrowing access in the US Dollar bond market, but needs €500M to build a factory in Germany. Simultaneously, a German industrial company can borrow cheaply in Euros, but needs \$550M to fund US operations. By issuing bonds in their respective home markets and entering a Cross-Currency Swap, both companies lower their all-in borrowing costs while matching the currency of their debt to their foreign subsidiary cash flows.

## Structure
### The Three-Phase Cash Flow Mechanics of a Cross-Currency Swap

```
5-Year EUR / USD Cross-Currency Swap Lifecycle (Spot S_0 = $1.10 / €1.00)
┌────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│ Phase 1: Initial Principal Exchange (Day 0, Initiation)│
│ • US Corp hands $550M USD to Swap Bank                 │
│ • Swap Bank hands €500M EUR to US Corp                 │
│   (Exchanged at current Spot FX rate — zero FX risk)   │
└───────────────────────────┬────────────────────────────┘
                            │
                            ▼
┌────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│ Phase 2: Periodic Interest Exchanges (Years 1 to 5)    │
│ • US Corp pays EUR interest (e.g., 3.5% on €500M)      │
│   using cash generated by its German subsidiary        │
│ • Swap Bank pays USD interest (e.g., 5.0% on $550M)    │
│   which US Corp uses to pay its domestic USD bondholders│
└───────────────────────────┬────────────────────────────┘
                            │
                            ▼
┌────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│ Phase 3: Terminal Principal Re-Exchange (Year 5 Maturity)│
│ • US Corp returns €500M EUR principal to Swap Bank     │
│ • Swap Bank returns $550M USD principal to US Corp     │
│   (Re-exchanged at the ORIGINAL initial Spot rate!)    │
└────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

### Valuation Formula as Two Currency Bonds
At any point $t$ during its life, the mark-to-market value of a Cross-Currency Swap receiving domestic currency ($B_D$) and paying foreign currency ($B_F$ at current spot rate $S_t$ domestic per foreign) is:
$$V_{\text{swap}} = B_D - S_t \cdot B_F$$

## Common Misconceptions
- **Misconception 1: "A Cross-Currency Swap and an Interest Rate Swap have the same counterparty credit risk."**  
  *Reality:* An Interest Rate Swap never exchanges notional principal—only net interest differentials are exchanged, so credit exposure is a small fraction of notional ($1\%-3\%$). A Cross-Currency Swap **physically exchanges 100% of the notional principal at maturity** at the historical Day-0 exchange rate! If the foreign currency moves by $30\%$ over a 10-year swap, the mark-to-market counterparty credit exposure on the terminal principal exchange is massive.
- **Misconception 2: "An FX Swap and a Cross-Currency Basis Swap are the same instrument."**  
  *Reality:* In dealer terminology, a short-term **FX Swap** consists only of a spot currency exchange paired with a single forward reversal (no interim coupon payments). A **Cross-Currency Basis Swap** is a multi-year floating-for-floating swap (e.g., 3M SOFR vs 3M EURIBOR + Basis) with periodic coupon exchanges and principal exchanges at start and maturity.
- **Misconception 3: "The Cross-Currency Basis is always zero."**  
  *Reality:* Due to global structural demand for US Dollar funding and bank balance sheet constraints, non-US borrowers routinely pay a negative **Cross-Currency Basis Spread** (e.g., EURIBOR $- 20\text{ bps}$ against flat USD SOFR) to secure synthetic USD funding.

## Notes for Future-Me
Use Cross-Currency Swaps for **Synthetic Foreign Debt Issuance**:
Even if your company has zero operations overseas, check global cross-currency basis spreads before issuing a large domestic corporate bond! Frequently, a US investment-grade corporation can issue a Euro-denominated bond in Europe ("Reverse Yankee Bond") and immediately swap 100% of the Euro proceeds and coupons back into fixed US Dollars via a Cross-Currency Swap at an all-in interest rate **15 to 30 basis points cheaper** than issuing a straight USD bond in New York.
